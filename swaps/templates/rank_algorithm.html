How the ballot allocates places

When the ballot closes, one automated draw allocates every place for the term. This page describes exactly what that draw does. It mirrors the code line by line, and the worked example below is re-checked against the real code by the site's automated tests.

The short version

  1. Everyone bids on their highest-ranked formal that still has room.
  2. If a formal has more bidders than seats, a random draw picks who gets in.
  3. Anyone who misses out immediately bids on their next choice that still has room, and so on.
  4. Nobody gets a second formal until everyone who could still get a first one has one. Then the leftovers are shared out the same way (round 2), then again (round 3), up to each person's limit.

1. What goes into the draw

  • Free seats per formal = the formal's places, minus places already held (including the swaps officer's seat when auto-attend is on), minus any released places still in their hold window.
  • Units. Each ballot group is one unit: its size is its number of accepted members (up to {{ max_group }}), it uses the leader's ranking and the leader's max-swaps limit. Everyone else is a unit of size 1. A group member's own ranking is ignored.
  • Each unit's list is its ranking, minus any formal that a member of the unit already holds.
  • Limit = "max swaps I'm happy to be assigned" (1–3, default 3). The draw never gives a unit more formals than this.
  • Only members with a verified email are included.
  • A fresh random seed (16 hex digits) is generated and saved alongside a log of every draw. Re-running with the same seed and data gives an identical result.

2. Flowchart

3. Exact pseudocode

# Inputs (see section 1)
free[f]  = free seats at formal f
list[u]  = unit u's ranking (best first), minus formals u already holds
size[u]  = 1 for a solo member, number of accepted members for a group
cap[u]   = max formals u may win (1–3)
rng      = random generator seeded with this run's recorded seed

repeat                                        # one pass = one ROUND
    won_this_round = {}
    out_this_round = {}
    progress = false
    loop                                      # one pass = one STEP
        bids = {}                             # formal -> units bidding for it
        for each unit u in fixed id order:
            if u in won_this_round or out_this_round
               or u already holds cap[u] formals:  skip u
            f = first formal in list[u] with free[f] ≥ size[u]
                and u does not already hold f
            if no such f:  add u to out_this_round
            else:          add u to bids[f]
        if bids is empty:  break                # this round is over

        for each formal f in bids:
            B = bids[f]
            if sum(size[u] for u in B) ≤ free[f]:
                winners = B                   # everyone fits: no draw
            else:
                order = rng.shuffle(B)        # THE LOTTERY (written to the log)
                winners = [];  left = free[f]
                for u in order:
                    if size[u] ≤ left:
                        winners += u;  left -= size[u]
                    # else: u is passed over (only possible for a group);
                    # a smaller unit later in the order may still fit
            for u in winners:
                free[f] -= size[u]
                u now holds f;  add u to won_this_round
                progress = true
        # losers are simply not in won_this_round, so on the next step
        # they bid again - their "first viable choice" now skips anything full
until progress == false                     # a whole round with no winners

every member of each winning unit gets a place at that formal

Within a step, each unit bids on exactly one formal, so formals are settled independently. The order formals are processed in doesn't change who wins.

5. What this means for you

  • Your 1st choice is always tried first, in the very first step, no matter what else you rank. The only exception: if it doesn't have enough free seats for you (or your group) before the draw starts, you begin with your 2nd.
  • A 1st choice beats a 2nd choice. A formal can fill up with people who ranked it 1st before those who ranked it 2nd get to bid (Cat and B above). Rank in true order of preference, and put popular formals first only if you really want them most.
  • Fairness rounds. Round 2 (a second formal) only starts once round 1 can't place anybody else, and round 3 likewise. You end up with nothing only if every formal you ranked filled up (for a group: never had enough seats for the whole group).
  • Groups win or lose together. If there's a draw and the group's turn comes when too few seats are left, it's passed over at that formal and tries its next choice in the next step. A smaller unit drawn after it can still take the leftover seats.
  • Your limit is respected. Once you hold your "max swaps" number of formals from this draw, you stop bidding. (It doesn't stop you claiming released places later.)
  • It's auditable. Every draw is recorded with its seed. Re-running the same seed on the same data reproduces the result exactly.
  • After the ballot, places freed by cancellations are released separately: first to members who missed out entirely, then to everyone.
